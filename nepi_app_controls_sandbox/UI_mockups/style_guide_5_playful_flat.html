Style Guide 05

Playful Flat

Bright saturated colors, big rounded cards, and a friendly rounded sans typeface — a consumer-app energy borrowed from smart-home and fitness apps, aimed at making a technical type-survey demo approachable to a non-engineer visiting the RUI for the first time.

Principle

The other four guides all assume an engineer or technician audience. This one assumes the opposite: someone unfamiliar with ROS topics or control types who just wants to know "what does this switch do and is it on." Every control gets a card of its own with generous whitespace, a bright single-purpose color, and large touch targets — buttons have a visible "pressed" ledge (an offset shadow) rather than a flat click target, which reads as more inviting and game-like. This is the most colorful and highest-personality option of the five; it is intentionally the furthest from NEPI's current utilitarian look.

Rules

— Every card gets its own rounded (18–20px), drop-shadowed surface — nothing is a flat inline row.
— Buttons use a solid "ledge" shadow (offset flat color, not blur) so they read as physically pressable.
— Each functional group gets its own accent hue (coral = actions, sky = selection, mint = confirmed/success, grape = brand/heading) rather than one accent color for everything.
— Copy stays plain-language first, with the technical field name (e.g. "demo_float_slider") demoted to small print, not the headline label.
— Corner radius, shadow, and color are all more saturated/exaggerated than any of the other four guides — this is the deliberate "friendly extreme" of the set.
Playful Flat covers the same 18 demo fields (10 controls + 8 data) as the real app. See concept_5_playful_flat.html and UI_mockups/README.md for the full layout and rationale.
